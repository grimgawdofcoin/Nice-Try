## The one constraint that shapes everything

Florida's cottage food law lets you produce and sell from home with no permit and no inspection — but only if you stay under $250,000/year gross sales, sell direct-to-consumer only (no wholesale), and ship within Florida. Interstate shipping is generally prohibited under cottage food. Your dry cacao qualifies because it is not a temperature-controlled (TCS) food.

What this means: start Florida-only and cheap. The "ship nationwide" version of the drop requires graduating to a co-packer or licensed commercial kitchen later (Phase 7). Don't let that stop you starting now.

Source: FL Statute 500.80; FDACS Cottage Foods; UF/IFAS guidance (links below).

## Phase 0 — Confirm legality & your lane (Week 1)

- Read FL Statute 500.80 and the FDACS Cottage Foods page. Confirm a dry, shelf-stable cacao block/powder fits (it is non-TCS, like candy and dry mixes, which are permitted).
- Call or email FDACS to confirm your specific product (ceremonial cacao block/powder, possibly spiced) is treated as a cottage food. Get the answer in writing if you can.
- Decide your lane: (A) Cottage food, Florida-only, $0 permit — fastest start. (B) Plan toward a co-packer for national shipping once demand is proven. **Start with A.**
- Check Broward County / City of Plantation for a Local Business Tax Receipt (most FL counties require one; budget ~$50–150).

Source: FL Statute 500.80; FDACS; UF/IFAS Cottage Food Operations PDF.

## Phase 1 — Source the cacao (Weeks 1–3)

Email these Dominican-origin suppliers. Request: ceremonial-grade confirmation, organic/kosher certs, price per kg, minimum order quantity (MOQ), lead time, and lot traceability. You want small MOQs so you can buy after pre-orders close.

- **Tabal Chocolate** (Milwaukee, USA) — makes ceremonial-grade cacao liquor from DR certified-organic beans; wholesale for business accounts; ships worldwide. Strong primary candidate.
- **Kahkow USA** (Brooklyn) — Dominican brand; organic cacao powder/products; wholesale via info@kahkow.com; US pickup/shipping.
- **Cocoa Family Farms** (DR) — organic cacao paste, powder, nibs; decades of wholesale supply to US manufacturers.
- **Santiago Cocoa** (DR) — USDA-organic/kosher beans and powder; samples on request; better for larger ocean-freight volumes later.

Design your serving around real ceremony norms: a ceremonial serving is roughly 42g, a lighter "meditation" serving ~28g, daily use ~7g. Prep is simple: warm water or plant milk, melt in the paste, blend, then sweeten/spice to taste — this becomes your ritual card copy.

Source: Tabal; Kahkow; Cocoa Family; Santiago Cocoa; Superfood Ceremony (serving sizes & prep).

## Phase 2 — Product, label & packaging (Weeks 2–4)

- Finalize ONE hero cacao (single-origin DR) plus, optionally, ONE signature spiced blend. No more.
- Build a compliant label. Florida cottage food + federal rules (21 CFR 101) require ALL of the following:
  - Name and physical address of the cottage food operation
  - Product name
  - Ingredients listed by weight (include sub-ingredients of any blend)
  - Allergen declaration (flag milk, soy, tree nuts, etc. if present or cross-contact)
  - Net weight in dual units (e.g., Net Wt. 250g / 8.8 oz)
  - This exact statement, ≥10-point type, in a contrasting color: *"Made in a cottage food operation that is not subject to Florida's food safety regulations."*
  - English (additional languages optional). No health/nutrition claims unless you add full federal nutrition labeling.
- Packaging v1: kraft pouch or craft box + printed label + hand-numbered stamp + a small ritual/brewing card. Cents per unit. Upgrade to premium vessels only from drop profit.

Source: FL Statute 500.80(3); FDACS; 21 CFR Part 101 (FDA labeling).

## Phase 3 — Set up the business (Weeks 2–4, parallel)

- Form a Florida LLC on Sunbiz: $125 total ($100 Articles of Organization + $25 registered-agent designation). Serve as your own registered agent to avoid extra fees. Posts in ~2–5 business days.
- Get an EIN free from the IRS (irs.gov, ~10 minutes). Never pay a service for this.
- Open a business bank account (keeps personal and business funds separate — preserves liability protection).
- Calendar the annual report: $138.75, due May 1 every year. Miss it and a $400 late fee applies; ignore it through mid-September and the LLC is dissolved.
- File your Local Business Tax Receipt with the county/city.
- Get food product-liability insurance quotes (specialty programs like the Food Liability Insurance Program exist for small/cottage food sellers). Carry it before you scale.

Source: Florida Division of Corporations (Sunbiz); IRS (EIN); ZenBusiness / CreditDonkey FL fee breakdowns.

## Phase 5 — Sell the first 30–50 by hand (Weeks 4–8)

This is your real validation — and the testers pay you, the only feedback that counts.

- Miami/Broward farmers & wellness markets, yoga / pilates / meditation studios, salons, popups, Dominican community events, friends' networks.
- Market rule to verify: as a cottage operator you sell direct to the consumer, but you generally cannot sell *through* or co-located with a licensed/regulated food vendor. Confirm each market's rules with the organizer and FDACS guidance first.
- Capture every buyer's email into The Court. Photograph everything. Ask for a one-line testimonial.

Source: FDACS / UF-IFAS cottage food guidance (market & event sales).

## Phase 6 — Run Drop 01 (Weeks 8–10)

- Announce Drop 01 to The Court: named, numbered, limited (~150 units), 48–72-hour pre-order window, with a real reason for the limit (single-origin lot).
- Take payment up front. THEN order inventory from your supplier.
- Blend, pack with ritual presentation, hand-check the first batch, ship within Florida.
- Follow up: request reviews and photos; offer a small post-drop reserve to anyone who missed it.

## Phase 7 — Cadence & the national-shipping decision (Months 3–12)

- Run a drop every 6–8 weeks. Each one a little bigger. Reinvest margin: better sourcing → better packaging → wider reach.
- The graduation point: to ship beyond Florida or sell wholesale, you must move off cottage food to a licensed commercial kitchen or a co-packer/contract manufacturer. Plan this only once demand is proven — customer pre-orders will fund the transition.
- Only after 3–4 successful drops: consider a second SKU, a standing-order/subscription, or a ceremony-kit upsell.

This works only if it's your one creative build for the next twelve months. Day job + Network+ as the stable floor; this gets the build energy; everything else parked. Drops don't ship on cadence if your attention is split five ways.
